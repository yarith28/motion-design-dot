## Mechanics and mode review

| Game | Defining transition and finite ending | Optional mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Last Contact | The only move replaces an orthogonally adjacent enemy with the moving piece, leaving its source empty. Every action destroys a future contact, so preserving your final capture while cutting the opponent's matters more than material advantage. Three finite study plans: 14/8/10 native actions. | Match series; Fixed 4×4 field, twelve pieces and fixed depth-three tactical house response. |
| Triad and Tower | Make a mandatory ray capture using a stack at least as tall as its victim, then optionally capture again or merge onto a friendly stack. Preserve an exposed top of each of three army types: stacking strengthens one attacker but can bury your last visible type and lose immediately. Three finite study plans: 8/8/8 native actions. | Match series; Nineteen cells, eighteen initial pieces and a bounded complete-turn opponent heuristic. |
| Crossing Court | Pieces have movement ranks but belong to whoever controls the half of the board they currently occupy. Capturing across the boundary can hand the moved piece to your rival for a countercapture; score captured rank value before either court empties. Three finite study plans: 22/24/28 native actions. | Match series; Fixed 4×6 court, eighteen pieces,48-turn settlement and bounded threat-aware rival. |
| Chromatic Command | A pawn slides forward along clear rays; the color of its landing square forces the opponent to use their pawn of that color. A blocked forced pawn passes control using the color under it, so movement chooses the opponent's next move family. Three finite study plans: 4/8/8 native actions. | Match series; Five colors and pawns per side; forward motion and blocked-cycle settlement keep races finite. |
| Outward and Home | Choose which lane runner advances its individual distance; on reaching the far edge it turns and uses a different return speed. Crossing an enemy resets that enemy to its current leg's starting edge and jumps beyond it, so timing intersections can outweigh raw distance. Three finite study plans: 23/22/25 native actions. | Match series; Five runners per side, speed 1–3, target 4 and90-turn settlement. |
| Pair Breaker | Place a stone to create a five-stone line or bracket exactly two enemies between the new stone and an older one. Captured pairs reopen cells and can dismantle a threatened line; either alignment or enough captured pairs wins. Three finite study plans: 2/3/3 native actions. | Match series; Fixed 7×7, at most 80 player turns and bounded tactical rival. |
| Looping Guard | Reposition by adjacent steps, but capture only by following an unobstructed orthogonal route that has traversed at least one corner loop. Empty loops can turn a remote blocker into a capture shield or expose a distant piece. Three finite study plans: 8/6/6 native actions. | Match series; Fixed 6×6 loop graph, eight pieces and 48-turn settlement. |
| Mill and Flight | Deploy reserves to a connected mill graph, then move along its edges; completing a three-point mill earns a selective removal. At three surviving pieces the movement rule changes to flying, so depletion can create a last tactical resource. Three finite study plans: 5/8/5 native actions. | Match series; 24 points, nine-piece pools per side, repetition and 70-turn settlement. |
| Mirrored Offensive | First make a non-pushing passive move on one of your home boards; then reuse its exact displacement on a board of the other color for an aggressive move that can push one enemy off an edge. Choose a passive move that licenses the needed attack. Three finite study plans: 16/20/12 native actions. | Match series; Four 4×4 fields, three stones per side per field and 60-turn settlement. |
| Marble Vault | Build a shared supported sphere pyramid using finite personal reserves, or promote an uncovered lower sphere to a supported higher socket. Completing an own square permits reclaiming up to two uncovered own spheres, making recycling and support denial part of the race to the apex. Three finite study plans: 16/11/6 native actions. | Match series; Thirty sockets in four layers, fifteen-sphere personal pools and 80-turn settlement. |
| Brimline Reserves | Insert a reserve at the edge of a hex field and push a whole occupied row toward its first gap. A completed own line can be removed with all connected row stones: recover your own reserves, permanently capture enemies, and keep enough reserve to take another turn. Three finite study plans: 12/10/9 native actions. | Match series; Nineteen hexes, seven-piece personal pools and 60-turn settlement. |
| Last Defense | Attack with a card, defend each attack with a higher same-suit card or trump, and add attacks only using ranks already on the table. Taking the whole table changes hands and attack initiative; drawing order determines who can shed after the deck ends. Three finite study plans: 9/7/6 native actions. | Match series; Two suits, ranks 1–6, four-card refill and 80-volley settlement. |
| Covered Columns | Draw or take the exposed discard, replace one card in a partly concealed two-row grid, or reveal a hidden card while discarding. A matching vertical pair cancels its score, and revealing your final card forces the opponent's final turn. Three finite study plans: 8/8/8 native actions. | Match series; Six cells per side, 24-card pool, finite deck and 16-turn settlement. |
| Double Written | Write each move tentatively in two empty squares. When these linked marks close a cycle, the opponent chooses one endpoint to make classical, forcing the connected component to collapse consistently. Winning lines depend on that collapse choice and move priority. Three finite study plans: 6/6/8 native actions. | Match series; Nine cells and finite numbered link/collapse play. |
| Capital Lines | Advance armies through owned territory while maintaining a connected supply path to the capital. Cutting an enemy link marks isolated armies for attrition on their next turn; restoring a route, capturing a relay or attacking a capital competes with immediate local gains. Three finite study plans: 9/10/10 native actions. | Match series; Fixed 5×5 field, three starting armies and three relays per side, 60-turn settlement. |
| Bracket and Effort | Set a progressive marginal tax schedule, then workers independently choose effort from their after-tax wage and convex effort cost. Revenue must fund dated services while disposable income preserves next-period training, so higher rates can reduce both work and future productivity. Three finite study plans: 16/16/16 native actions. | Challenge stream; Three workers, four periods, three marginal brackets and bounded wages/costs. |
| Club Tariff | Set a one-time membership fee and a per-use price. New customers compare total consumer surplus with the fee, while existing members treat that fee as sunk and choose only marginal usage; fee commitment, capacity and successive arriving cohorts change the best tariff over time. Three finite study plans: 7/7/7 native actions. | Challenge stream; Six possible arriving members, three periods, bounded quadratic usage and prices 0–6. |
| Pooling Desk | Quote one premium/deductible contract to a population whose risk groups decide whether to enroll. The price changes the composition of the pool before claims are paid; maintain coverage and protection while avoiding the high-risk-only insolvency trap. Three finite study plans: 9/9/9 native actions. | Challenge stream; Three risk groups, three periods, one uniform premium/deductible and finite reserve. |
| Network Tipping | Invest in durable platform quality or subsidize new adopters. Each cohort compares intrinsic appeal, switching cost and its neighbors' prior adoption; early critical-mass spending can sustain later adoption, but poorly placed subsidies expire without creating a stable network. Three finite study plans: 15/15/15 native actions. | Challenge stream; Six-node degree-two peer graph, five periods, quality cap six and rival cap three. |
| Bonus and Burden | Write a base wage and two performance bonuses, then an agent chooses costly quantity, real quality or metric manipulation. One scarce audit can remove proxy gaming, while induced practice changes later effort costs; high measured output can still leave the principal worse off. Three finite study plans: 15/15/15 native actions. | Challenge stream; Three projects, one audit, bounded quantity/quality/gaming and explicit practice cost floor. |

## Scope and limits

The browser suite uses test-only source-informed legal plans through normal public controls and public replay seeds. Card plans inspect deterministic concealed/deck state during test preparation; they prove reachable outcomes and recovery, not information-limited optimal play or unaided human success probability. Every desktop game action uses focus and Enter. Every touch game action uses touchscreen taps; native mode selection is setup before Begin.

Testing uses Chromium and emulated touch at 390px, with 320px responsive checks; it does not establish physical-device, other-browser-engine or screen-reader coverage. Late-stage evidence is guarded-model generation/property work; the five economies additionally complete sixteen whole generated model studies each. It does not claim full browser wins at stage 10003. Independent source review and ordinary native samples are recorded separately by the catalogue auditor.
