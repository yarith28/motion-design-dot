# Independent depth and playability review

Status: final written-design review. Design review only; no implementation or tested quality claim.

Reviewed the 200-entry baseline inventory, EXPANSION-200-DESIGNS.md, EXPANSION-200-REVIEW.md, and current Safe Return, Convoy Ledger, Quiet Foil and Seed Tomorrow implementations. No repository AGENTS.md was found. The baseline illustrates why a named mechanic and a reachable terminal state are insufficient: separable daily choices, weak all-or-nothing allocation, optional parrying and linear cellular XOR can conceal shallow or duplicated play.

## Review contract

Each candidate receives a disposition and a concrete release gate. `Conditional` means the written design has a defensible coupled decision loop, not that implementation is authorized or certified. `Revise` means a specific defect prevents acceptance. `Reject` means a proposed loop is shallow or duplicate without material redesign.

Every conditional candidate inherits the README ordinary-play contract: visible-rule first review without solutions/probes, legitimate public-input win and natural failure, keyboard-only and touch-only complete run, replay with changed decisions, actual invalid-action handling, restart, focus and activation checks. Puzzle failure may be a visibly invalid submission or ordinary dead end with explained recovery; no artificial “lose now” button counts. Competitive losses do not establish attainable wins. Timing modes must disclose limitations and offer adjustable practice without misrepresenting it as equivalent competitive access.

A game-specific gate must prove its defining choice matters. Explicitly challenge first legal, highest immediate reward, constant action, wait, independent per-control optimization, and a short memorized cycle as relevant. These policy challenges supplement human review rather than prove depth. Require at least one visible counterexample to the strongest tempting policy and a reachable, understandable better choice.

Final remediation baseline is pending. Re-review all changed baseline mechanics before implementation, particularly the known systems, discovery, XOR and fencing changes.

## Review outcome

100 primary candidates (A14 + B35 + C24 + D27) pass the **written design** depth gate conditionally. A05 Hinge Atlas is withdrawn as overlapping C13 articulated configuration planning; C16 Call and Return is rejected for recall-only play; A15 Nested Rooms remains revise-reserve because its later nested-state coupling has no concrete authored witness; A17 Two Little Loops remains revise-reserve until simultaneous execution order is specified. Neither is counted. No unresolved `revise` candidate is counted. All games still require ordinary-play proof, difficulty calibration, accessibility verification and comparison against the parent's remediated 200-game baseline.

Concrete defects changed the proposals: D04 became construction of race-condition witnesses instead of unsupported serial-time penalties; D22 fixed-protocol repetition became causally consistent snapshot reconstruction; D17 corrected a false sliding-window premise with an explicit non-greedy parsing fixture; A08 now requires a meaningful flip-bound challenge; C06 removed fictitious terminal stranding/concession evidence; C09 separates free collision preview from warned damaging commitment; A15 clarified finite unique-parent dual-door geometry but was withheld because the intro only proves a movable bridge; B15 and B28 gained defined termination; B29's withdrawn race became reactive hidden-fugitive interception. Rejected drafts are not represented as tested failures or implemented changes.

Highest remaining risks: A03 knot topology/editor accessibility; A15 useful multi-level containment beyond its intro; C07/C08/C09/C13/C21/C23/C24 geometry and simulation fidelity; B16 literal-partner perfect completion; D26 hidden-payoff fairness; opponent strength and hidden-state isolation in competitive games; academic D-family discoverability without instructional overload. A named tutorial or known rule system is insufficient: the per-ID gate must survive ordinary first play and expose the intended decision contrast.

Authored fixture traces are design witnesses and teaching material, **not** unaided verification. The independent first-play reviewer should receive fresh equivalent challenge instances without seeing these answer traces, then use only displayed rules and public controls. Failure evidence may be a natural recoverable puzzle dead end or explained failed certificate; a disabled invalid click or a surrender button alone does not count.
