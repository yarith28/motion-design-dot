# Expansion to500: inventory and distinct-game plan

The final target is500 unique games:200 additions to the preserved300-game baseline at `c96ec5e39669d330ac761d98a335afe44c34a977`. The first 100 additions are the five batches in [the 400-game plan](EXPANSION-400-DESIGNS.md). Their400-game release is a checked milestone within this larger task. The next 100 are planned below; these proposal rows are not a claim of implementation or publication.

All 100 proposed IDs are unique and distinct from the current400 catalog IDs. Each proposal defines actual player choices/state transitions, three finite studies, real controls, loss/recovery, nearest-source differences and an optional-mode generation/witness strategy. The [independent source-comparison matrix](design500/proposal-review.json) records accept/revise/reject decisions against actual400 engines and new-to-new overlaps. Implementation and procedural-mode support remain subject to real source, rules and full desktop/touch verification.

Five isolated20-game scopes reuse the stable Journey presentation/lifecycle while owning independent rules. Root coordinates catalog/evidence/commit/publication; existing rooms, Kitchen Cats and the motion showcase remain preserved. All workers retain the user-selected inherited GPT-6.1 Sol Ultra settings.

Review has already rejected weighted resident-location replacement, ordinary Sokoban, token redistribution, rigidity diagnosis, ribbon/surface gluing, generic grounded proof and direct vertex editing when their decisions overlapped existing/proposed games. Replacements and substantive revisions are recorded in the proposal artifacts; title/theme differences alone do not establish a new game.

## civic-room —20 games

| Game | Defining decisions |
|---|---|
| Looping Guard (`looping-guard`) | Reposition by adjacent steps, but capture only by following an unobstructed orthogonal route that has traversed at least one corner loop. Empty loops can turn a remote blocker into a capture shield or expose a distant piece. |
| Chromatic Command (`chromatic-command`) | A pawn slides forward along clear rays; the color of its landing square forces the opponent to use their pawn of that color. A blocked forced pawn passes control using the color under it, so movement chooses the opponent's next move family. |
| Mirrored Offensive (`mirrored-offensive`) | First make a non-pushing passive move on one of your home boards; then reuse its exact displacement on a board of the other color for an aggressive move that can push one enemy off an edge. Choose a passive move that licenses the needed attack. |
| Pair Breaker (`pair-breaker`) | Place a stone to create a five-stone line or bracket exactly two enemies between the new stone and an older one. Captured pairs reopen cells and can dismantle a threatened line; either alignment or enough captured pairs wins. |
| Mill and Flight (`mill-and-flight`) | Deploy reserves to a connected mill graph, then move along its edges; completing a three-point mill earns a selective removal. At three surviving pieces the movement rule changes to flying, so depletion can create a last tactical resource. |
| Last Contact (`last-contact`) | The only move replaces an orthogonally adjacent enemy with the moving piece, leaving its source empty. Every action destroys a future contact, so preserving your final capture while cutting the opponent's matters more than material advantage. |
| Brimline Reserves (`brimline-reserves`) | Insert a reserve at the edge of a hex field and push a whole occupied row toward its first gap. A completed own line can be removed with all connected row stones: recover your own reserves, permanently capture enemies, and keep enough reserve to take another turn. |
| Last Defense (`last-defense`) | Attack with a card, defend each attack with a higher same-suit card or trump, and add attacks only using ranks already on the table. Taking the whole table changes hands and attack initiative; drawing order determines who can shed after the deck ends. |
| Covered Columns (`covered-columns`) | Draw or take the exposed discard, replace one card in a partly concealed two-row grid, or reveal a hidden card while discarding. A matching vertical pair cancels its score, and revealing your final card forces the opponent's final turn. |
| Double Written (`double-written`) | Write each move tentatively in two empty squares. When these linked marks close a cycle, the opponent chooses one endpoint to make classical, forcing the connected component to collapse consistently. Winning lines depend on that collapse choice and move priority. |
| Outward and Home (`outward-and-home`) | Choose which lane runner advances its individual distance; on reaching the far edge it turns and uses a different return speed. Crossing an enemy resets that enemy to its current leg's starting edge and jumps beyond it, so timing intersections can outweigh raw distance. |
| Crossing Court (`crossing-court`) | Pieces have movement ranks but belong to whoever controls the half of the board they currently occupy. Capturing across the boundary can hand the moved piece to your rival for a countercapture; score captured rank value before either court empties. |
| Marble Vault (`marble-vault`) | Build a shared supported sphere pyramid using finite personal reserves, or promote an uncovered lower sphere to a supported higher socket. Completing an own square permits reclaiming up to two uncovered own spheres, making recycling and support denial part of the race to the apex. |
| Capital Lines (`capital-lines`) | Advance armies through owned territory while maintaining a connected supply path to the capital. Cutting an enemy link marks isolated armies for attrition on their next turn; restoring a route, capturing a relay or attacking a capital competes with immediate local gains. |
| Bracket and Effort (`bracket-and-effort`) | Set a progressive marginal tax schedule, then workers independently choose effort from their after-tax wage and convex effort cost. Revenue must fund dated services while disposable income preserves next-period training, so higher rates can reduce both work and future productivity. |
| Club Tariff (`club-tariff`) | Set a one-time membership fee and a per-use price. New customers compare total consumer surplus with the fee, while existing members treat that fee as sunk and choose only marginal usage; fee commitment, capacity and successive arriving cohorts change the best tariff over time. |
| Pooling Desk (`pooling-desk`) | Quote one premium/deductible contract to a population whose risk groups decide whether to enroll. The price changes the composition of the pool before claims are paid; maintain coverage and protection while avoiding the high-risk-only insolvency trap. |
| Network Tipping (`network-tipping`) | Invest in durable platform quality or subsidize new adopters. Each cohort compares intrinsic appeal, switching cost and its neighbors' prior adoption; early critical-mass spending can sustain later adoption, but poorly placed subsidies expire without creating a stable network. |
| Bonus and Burden (`bonus-and-burden`) | Write a base wage and two performance bonuses, then an agent chooses costly quantity, real quality or metric manipulation. One scarce audit can remove proxy gaming, while induced practice changes later effort costs; high measured output can still leave the principal worse off. |
| Triad and Tower (`triad-and-tower`) | Make a mandatory ray capture using a stack at least as tall as its victim, then optionally capture again or merge onto a friendly stack. Preserve an exposed top of each of three army types: stacking strengthens one attacker but can bury your last visible type and lose immediately. |
